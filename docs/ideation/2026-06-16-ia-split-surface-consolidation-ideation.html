Consolidating Split Surfaces

Two reports — "watch and archive lead to practically the same place" and "move the directory-listing settings under the account page" — are the same anti-pattern: one logical thing split across two pages. This set treats them together.

Grounding Context

Codebase context. web-temple is an Express 4 + EJS server-rendered MPA (CommonJS, Postgres + Redis, no bundler/framework). Two independent "this splits one thing across two pages" cases surfaced:

Video: /watch vs /archive

/watch ("Past Services") is public (routes/watch.js), a Facebook-iframe lightbox over a curated placeholder list (or the dormant Facebook Graph source) — no search, no detail page, no chat. /archive ("Recording Archive") is members-only (routes/recordings.js, requireAuth), DB-backed, with keyword/Torah-portion/service-type/date filters, a per-recording page with a native <video> player + captions, and an "Archived Chat History" replay. A logged-in member sees both "Watch" and "Archive" in the nav (layout.ejs:106, 110-111) with no copy distinguishing them. Both currently run on fake data, so they look like twins. The split was a deliberate 2-day-old decision (public showcase vs members' annotated library) — but invisible in the labels, and amplified by the empty state.

Settings: /account/settings vs /account/directory

/account/settings ("Account Settings") holds Profile, Notification Preferences, and Change Password. /account/directory ("My Directory Listing") holds phone, address, birthday, household, interests, the per-field visibility flags, and the "list me" opt-in. Both are "manage my own profile," yet they are two separate pages — and /account/settings does not link to the directory editor at all. The editor is reachable only from the public /directory browse (directory/index.ejs:3,9,52). So a member can't reach their own privacy settings from their Account. The public /directory (viewing other members) is a genuinely separate concern and stays separate.

External grounding. Surveyed synagogues (Central, Park Avenue, Touro, Adas Israel) keep service archives public, gating member features via a separate "Member Portal," and well-designed video products (PBS Passport, member hubs) use one browse surface with item-level locks rather than two near-identical pages. NN/g: one function per nav item; verb-first concrete labels; co-locate a data field with its visibility control. Streaming-license law covers live + archived worship but not commercial music — a legal rationale for "clips public / full service gated" distinct from member-value.

Topic Axes

  • Navigation & labeling — duplicate or ambiguous nav entries (Watch + Archive both shown; Account has no path to listing config) and labels that don't carry the distinction.
  • Surface consolidation & structure — one surface vs two; sections + anchor-nav vs tabs; browse vs detail; the artifacts of a page that used to stand alone.
  • Separation of concerns — what genuinely should stay split (the public browse of others; public-vs-member access) vs what shouldn't (my profile vs my directory fields; two browse tiers of the same videos).
  • Population & data model — the empty/fake-data state that makes pages look like duplicates; auto-ingestion; one canonical source.
  • Discoverability & value — making settings and features findable and their value legible (where my settings live; why the member tier is worth signing in for).

1.

Adopt a "one surface per intent" consolidation pattern

Separation of concernscross-cutting
Description

Both reported problems are one anti-pattern: a single user intent split across two pages. Treat them as instances of one rule — one surface per intent; differentiate by access, recency, or enrichment at the section/item level, not by spawning a parallel page — and adopt it as a lightweight IA convention so the next feature doesn't repeat it.

Basis

reasoned: the video pair (Watch + Archive in nav) and the settings pair (settings + directory) share the identical structure — two top-level surfaces for one intent — and the external prescription is the same for both (PBS Passport item-level gating; the "Member Hub" pattern; NN/g one-function-per-nav-item).

Rationale

The most valuable output here isn't fixing two pages — it's naming the recurring pattern. A one-line convention prevents re-litigating "why are there two?" on the next surface, and it's the lens that makes ideas 2–9 cohere instead of reading as nine unrelated tweaks.

Downsides

A principle is not a fix; it needs the concrete instances below to land. Over-applied it could collapse concerns that should stay separate — the public /directory browse of others is not "my settings," and the public /watch showcase has a real reason to exist apart from the member archive.

Confidence80%
ComplexityLow
2.

Merge the directory editor into Account Settings as a fourth section

Surface consolidation & structureaccount/directory
Description

Move the /account/directory editor into /account/settings as a fourth <section> ("Directory Listing & Privacy"), delete the standalone view, and keep the PUT /api/account/directory API. Keep it one scrollable SSR page with a top anchor-nav (Profile · Notifications · Directory Listing · Password) — not tabs, which add JS state and history handling for no functional gain in a no-bundler MPA.

Basis

direct: settings.ejs:7–73 is three section.settings-section blocks each saving via a JSON API with an in-place form-message; directory-listing.ejs:1 already uses container.account-settings + section.settings-section on the same /css/account.css — structurally isomorphic to a fourth section.

Rationale

Directly answers "no reason to split my own settings across two pages," at the lowest complexity: one view deleted, one section added, no new routes or shell. A member can move from name/email to phone/visibility without leaving the page.

Downsides

The page gets long, so the anchor-nav (idea 4) is load-bearing. The editor ships heavier widgets (household modal, tag-stack, birthday selects) than the simple settings forms — fine, since its JS is defer-loaded and gated on #directoryForm, but page weight grows. The deeper version — co-locating name/email with their visibility toggles — tangles two save APIs and is out of scope here.

Confidence90%
ComplexityLow–Medium
3.

Stop redirecting the listing save to /directory; keep the member in Account

Surface consolidation & structureaccount/directory
Description

On a successful save, directory-listing.js:332 hard-redirects to the public /directory browse — an artifact of the editor being a standalone page. Once it's a section under Account, that bounces the member out of their settings. Replace it with an in-place success message + scroll-to-section, matching the other three settings forms.

Basis

direct: the redirect lives in the client script directory-listing.js:332 (not the controller — the PUT returns JSON); the existing settings forms show #profileMessage/#preferencesMessage in place rather than navigating away.

Rationale

A one-line change that removes a disorienting bounce and enables multi-step editing — save phone, then update address — without leaving the page.

Downsides

Members who arrived from the public /directory might expect to return there; landing them on the section (idea 4's anchor) covers it. A referer-based conditional redirect would be over-engineering.

Confidence90%
ComplexityLow
4.

Preserve every entry point — and add the Account-side path that never existed

Discoverability & valueaccount/directory
Description

Three moves together: (a) 301-redirect /account/directory → /account/settings#directory-listing so bookmarks, the registration opt-in flow, and the R20 nudge keep working; (b) repoint the three /directory links ("Manage your own listing", "Add my listing", "Be the first…") to the new anchor; (c) add the Account-side path that's missing today — /account/settings has no link to directory config at all, so the only route to your privacy settings is via the public browse.

Basis

direct: directory/index.ejs:3,9,52 link to /account/directory; settings.ejs:3 subtitle is "Manage your profile, notifications, and password" — directory/privacy is unmentioned and unreachable from Account.

Rationale

Consolidation must not strand the legitimate "see other members → add myself" motivation or break deep links from registration/nudge emails — and it fixes the root discoverability bug: a member can't reach their own privacy controls from their Account page.

Downsides

Relies on a stable section id for the hash anchor; the redirect should be permanent (301) so search and bookmarks update.

Confidence90%
ComplexityLow
5.

Auto-draft a recording when a livestream ends

Population & data modelwatch/archive
Description

The strongest fix for "they look identical" isn't layout — it's population. /archive is effectively unused (manual publish only) and /watch shows 2–4 curated placeholders, so both display fake data and read as twins. Add a job that creates a draft recording from a finished stream's metadata, making manual publish additive (review → annotate → publish) instead of the only content path.

Basis

direct: there is no automated ingestion today; the recordings schema already carries provider_name/provider_recording_id implying an intended-but-unbuilt pipeline, and the admin list literally says "Check back after the next livestream finishes." Migration 012 already defines the draft → published state machine.

Rationale

Once /archive fills with real, annotated services, the genuine difference between the two surfaces (public showcase vs members' searchable, captioned, chat-replay library) becomes visible — much of the perceived redundancy is an empty-state artifact, not a structural one.

Downsides

Depends on the provider/stream integration (the Facebook Graph token isn't live), and ingestion must capture a playable reference — Facebook embeds are iframes, not direct media files, the same gap that limits unifying the two players (idea 8).

Confidence70%
ComplexityMedium–High
6.

Collapse the duplicate video nav to one "Watch" entry that deepens for members

Navigation & labelingwatch/archive
Description

Keep one always-present "Watch" entry; drop the separate "Archive" nav item and surface the members' archive from within /watch (a "Full member archive" section/link on the page). The page, not the nav, carries the access distinction — the PBS/Passport and member-hub shape.

Basis

direct: layout.ejs:106 ("Watch", always) and layout.ejs:110–111 ("Archive", logged-in only) put two top-level video links in one nav. external: well-designed video products keep one browse surface and enrich in place rather than adding a second nav item.

Rationale

Two nav items for one intent fractures attention and signals inconsistent IA (NN/g: one function per nav item). One entry forces the page to explain the value split — the right layer for that explanation.

Downsides

This partially reverses the deliberate 2-day-old "separate page" decision — worth an explicit team call. Fully surfacing the archive inside /watch runs into the source-join/player gap (idea 8); the nav/label change is cheap, the inline-archive part is not.

Confidence80%
ComplexityMedium
7.

Name the access distinction in the video labels

Navigation & labelingwatch/archive
Description

"Watch" and "Archive" are abstract near-synonyms. If two labels remain, name what actually differs: "Recent Services" (public) and "Member Archive" (members) — recency/access, not two words for the same video list. Avoid "Library", "On-Demand", "Media Center".

Basis

external: NN/g guidance — verb-first/concrete labels beat abstract nouns, "Library" reads as books to older users, and tabs/labels only disambiguate when they name the access distinction.

Rationale

A near-zero-cost comprehension win: even if the structural work waits, the labels stop reading as duplicates. Pairs naturally with idea 6.

Downsides

Labels alone don't fix the duplicate-surface structure; this is the cheap half of the nav work, not a substitute for it.

Confidence80%
ComplexityLow
8.

One video browse surface with item-level gating (PBS-Passport model)

Surface consolidation & structurewatch/archive
Description

The target end-state: one /watch grid where member-only items show an inline lock rather than living on a separate page; the gate is on items/features, not the page, so the URL stays shareable and indexable. The rich per-recording experience (native player, captions, chat replay) lives at the detail page members deep-link into.

Basis

external: PBS Passport's inline-lock grid and the broader pattern — "build one browse surface; gate items, not pages."

Rationale

Resolves the two-browse-tiers mental model structurally and demonstrates member value in place rather than describing it in copy.

Downsides

Real plumbing, and the verifier flagged it as the recurring under-estimate: /watch items are Facebook iframe embeds with no direct media file and no per-service join to the DB recordings, while /archive plays native <video> — one grid with two item types behind two players is substantial. Best treated as the north star that idea 5 (population) and a shared data model make reachable, not a quick win.

Confidence60%
ComplexityHigh
9.

Reframe why the archive is gated at all

Separation of concernswatch/archive
Description

Access is currently split "public showcase vs members-only" largely by inheritance. Two sharper rationales exist: (a) the streaming-license angle — edited/sermon clips public, full unedited services (with licensed music) gated, a legal basis not a membership wall; or (b) default to the prevailing synagogue norm (public service archive) and make members-only the deliberate exception it has to justify (privacy of identifiable congregants, building-security). Choose the rationale on purpose.

Basis

external: CCLI/WORSHIPcast licenses cover live + archived worship but not commercial music; surveyed synagogues keep service archives public and gate via a separate Member Portal rather than a wall on the video archive.

Rationale

Whether to merge or keep the video surfaces depends entirely on why they're separated. A clear rationale (license vs privacy vs member-value) tells you what can go public and what must stay gated — and therefore which of ideas 6–8 are even on the table.

Downsides

Requires the temple to confirm its music-licensing and privacy stance; the "default public" norm may not fit a small, security-conscious congregation, so this is a decision to surface, not a foregone conclusion.
